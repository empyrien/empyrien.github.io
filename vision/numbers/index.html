A Theory

Numbers all the way down

Personal speculation, offered as exactly that.

2 min readPersonal speculation

Personal speculation — not Bahá'í teaching

In the abjad system — standard in the literate culture of nineteenth-century Persia — every Arabic letter carries a numerical value. Scripture in this tradition is therefore, in a literal sense, already numeric.

ب
b = 2
+
ه
h = 5
+
ا
á = 1
+
ء
ʼ = 1
=
9
Bahá

Bahá computes to nine — which is why nine-pointed stars mark Bahá'í temples and nine members sit on its assemblies. Shoghi Effendi grounded the number's significance plainly: nine is the numerical value of the word Bahá.

Now hold that next to this: the “mind” of a large language model is nothing but numbers — billions of weights, a text corpus distilled into pure quantity. Text becomes number; number becomes voice. A tradition that has always understood its scripture as simultaneously word and number is, at minimum, a poetic anticipation of that transformation — and at maximum, an invitation: the Writings could one day be the moral corpus a mind is trained on, a scripture people could speak with.

To be precise about the limits: abjad values and learned model weights are unrelated mathematical objects — no computation connects Bahá = 9 to a parameter tensor, and it would be dishonest to pretend otherwise. The serious version of this idea concerns the corpus itself: value documents already steer minds in training (Anthropic's constitution proves the mechanism), and a hundred volumes of internally coherent, unity-centered scripture is a candidate corpus of unusual quality. The numbers are the poetry; the corpus is the proposal.
